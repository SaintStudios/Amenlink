Privacy policy

This policy explains personal data processing on amen-app.com and in the AMEN iOS app. It distinguishes website browsing from app features and explains what happens when you use accounts, faith personalization, Nema, audio, Bible Lock and paid access. The data involved depends on the features you use.

Key points

  • AI requests are sent to Google Gemini. Saved conversations and account data use Supabase.
  • RevenueCat processes purchase status. AppsFlyer receives app events and attribution data, including some onboarding selections.
  • Faith preferences and conversations can reveal sensitive information. Reading this policy does not give consent to process it.
  • Deleting the app or your AMEN account does not cancel an Apple subscription. Privacy requests go to amenapphilfe@gmail.com.

2. Website requests and hosting

This static website is served through GitHub Pages and its delivery infrastructure. A request necessarily exposes your IP address and technical information such as request time, requested URL, browser information and, when sent by your browser, the referring page. The hosting provider may keep operational and security logs.

The purposes are delivery, fault diagnosis and protection against abuse. Where the GDPR applies, the basis is Article 6(1)(f), our legitimate interest in operating a secure website. We do not claim that IP addresses are always anonymized or that all hosting logs are deleted after a specific number of days.

3. Cookies, language selection and external links

The legal pages do not add analytics, advertising pixels, external fonts, cookies or local storage. Automatic language selection reads your browser’s primary language on your device. Manual language selection is carried in the page URL. Unsupported languages fall back to English. No language preference is sent to AMEN as a separate analytics event.

Following an App Store or provider link connects you to that provider. Your browser then sends request information to that destination under its own privacy terms. An email link opens your email application; no message is sent until you send it.

4. Support and email correspondence

If you contact us, we process your email address, message, attachments and information needed to answer the request. The support address uses Gmail, so Google also provides email infrastructure. Avoid sending passwords, full payment details or unnecessary sensitive information.

Article 6(1)(b) applies to requests about your contract or pre-contractual questions; Article 6(1)(f) applies to other correspondence and handling complaints. Relevant correspondence may be retained to resolve the issue, meet legal duties or establish and defend claims. Sensitive information requires an additional lawful condition.

5. Accounts, authentication and profile

AMEN uses Supabase for authentication, database functions and file storage. Depending on your sign-in method and choices, data includes an account identifier, email address, authentication/session information, name, selected profile picture and account creation or update times. Sign in with Apple can provide an Apple identifier and a relay email address.

These data enable login, account recovery, synchronization and personalized features. The usual basis is Article 6(1)(b). Security and misuse prevention may rely on Article 6(1)(f); legally required records rely on Article 6(1)(c). Session credentials and preferences may also be stored on your device.

6. Reading activity, notes and personalization

The app processes language and Bible-version choices, reading activity, streaks, achievements, saved verses, highlights, notes and settings where the corresponding feature is used. Some data is stored locally; account-linked features can synchronize with Supabase. Profile or ranking features may show the name, picture or activity you use for those features.

The purpose is to provide your chosen reading, progress and personalization functions, usually under Article 6(1)(b). Notes, denominations and faith goals can reveal beliefs or personal circumstances and require the additional protection described in the sensitive-data section. Do not put confidential third-party information in shared profile fields.

7. Nema and other AI features

Nema is AMEN’s AI conversation interface. The current app sends requests to Google’s Gemini API; processing is not confined to AMEN’s own servers. Requests can include your message, loaded conversation context, name, response language, denomination and instructions used to personalize the answer. Title generation and explanations of Bible verses also send relevant text.

Some onboarding responses and Bible Lock reading recommendations use Google as well. Their requests can include your answers, chosen needs, reading constraints and recent passage references. Inputs and outputs may reveal intimate information. Do not provide passwords, payment details or another person’s confidential data. Provision of the requested feature normally uses Article 6(1)(b), with an additional condition for special-category data.

8. Google AI processing and model improvement

Google receives AI input and produces output under the terms applicable to AMEN’s API project. Its published Gemini terms distinguish paid and unpaid services: paid-service prompts and responses are not used to improve Google products, while unpaid-service rules can permit improvement and human review. Separate regional provisions also apply. See Google Gemini API terms.

The app source alone does not establish the project’s billing status, applicable provider settings or every provider retention period. We therefore do not make a universal promise of no provider training, no human access or zero retention. Provider security, abuse prevention and legal processing may continue even where model-improvement use is excluded.

9. Conversation history and deletion

Conversations, messages, responses and generated titles can be saved locally and in Supabase for continued access and context. Closing a conversation, closing the app or ending a session does not by itself delete saved history. This replaces any earlier description that chats are normally kept only for an active session.

Use available conversation controls or contact us to request deletion or a copy. A deletion request must also consider associated account records, service providers and backups; it does not automatically recall copies you exported or shared. Storage required for legal obligations or claims may continue with restricted use.

10. Read-aloud audio

When you request generated speech, the app sends the text to be read, voice settings and generation instructions to Google. This can include Bible text, a devotional or a prayer. Generated audio and voice information may be cached on your device for playback.

The current read-aloud implementation generates speech from text; it is not a microphone recording feature. The request’s storage setting is not a guarantee that Google keeps no operational, security or legal records. The basis for providing requested playback is generally Article 6(1)(b), with additional protection when the text contains sensitive personal data.

11. Bible Lock and Screen Time

Bible Lock uses Apple’s Family Controls and Screen Time frameworks when you authorize them. The app stores selected application/category/domain tokens, schedules, unlock settings and reading-session progress needed for the feature. Apple’s selection tokens are opaque identifiers; they are not a general permission to read your browsing history or messages.

AI reading recommendations can send your chosen need and passage constraints to Google as described above. Revoking Screen Time authorization in system settings affects the feature. The service processes feature settings under Article 6(1)(b); Apple controls its own framework processing. Device permissions do not themselves supply GDPR consent for unrelated analytics.

12. Profile photos, notifications and widgets

Choosing a profile photo uploads the selected image to Supabase storage when you save it. Image URLs can grant access to that image, so share them carefully. Notification features use permission state, notification preferences and, for remote delivery, a device push token with Apple’s push service and AMEN’s backend.

Widgets and app extensions can access shared app data needed to show content or implement Bible Lock. You can change photo, notification and Screen Time permissions in iOS settings. Denying a permission may limit its feature. Authorization for a device capability is distinct from consent to advertising or sensitive-data processing.

13. Purchases, Apple and RevenueCat

Apple handles App Store payment and billing. AMEN does not receive your full payment-card number from an Apple in-app purchase. RevenueCat helps validate transactions and manage access; it processes customer/app-user identifiers, products, entitlement and trial status, transaction information and related device or technical information.

RevenueCat can be linked to an AMEN account and AppsFlyer identifier. Purchase and subscription events may be used for attribution through configured integrations. Access administration usually relies on Article 6(1)(b), mandatory records on Article 6(1)(c), and optional marketing processing on consent where required. See Apple privacy policy and RevenueCat privacy policy.

14. AppsFlyer, app events and attribution

The app includes AppsFlyer for usage measurement and attribution. The current implementation reports onboarding steps, selected denomination, selected goals, Bible-version choices, paywall events and some purchase-related events. Technical data can include app version, timestamps, identifiers and permission state. RevenueCat and AppsFlyer identifiers can be linked for subscription attribution.

Apple’s tracking permission controls access to tracking allowed by Apple; denial does not necessarily stop every operational or app event. Attribution integrations may involve advertising partners. Optional tracking requires consent where applicable. Because denomination and goals can reveal religious beliefs, Article 6 legitimate interests alone is not sufficient for those data. See AppsFlyer privacy policy.

15. Religious beliefs and other sensitive data

Denomination, prayer, faith goals and conversations may reveal religious beliefs, health or other special categories of data under Article 9 GDPR. Such processing needs both an Article 6 basis and an applicable Article 9 condition, ordinarily explicit consent for optional faith personalization. Reading a policy, using the app or allowing Apple tracking is not that explicit consent.

You may request withdrawal, restriction or deletion at Email AMEN and change available preferences. Withdrawal does not invalidate earlier lawful processing. Avoid entering sensitive data about other people without authority. Disclosure of sensitive choices to analytics providers needs its own justification; this document cannot create consent or make an otherwise unlawful disclosure lawful.

16. Children and eligibility

An App Store age rating classifies content; it does not replace contract capacity, data-protection consent or an AI provider’s age rules. Google’s current Gemini API terms restrict services directed to, or likely accessed by, people under 18. AMEN’s Google-powered AI features are therefore intended for adults aged 18 or over.

For other features, parental authorization and local child-data rules may apply. Where consent is the basis, the GDPR’s child-consent threshold depends on national law. Contact us if you believe a child’s data was processed without the required authorization so we can investigate and arrange appropriate restriction or deletion.

17. Purposes and legal bases

The relevant bases depend on the purpose: Article 6(1)(b) for requested account, reading, AI and purchase-access services; Article 6(1)(c) for legal duties; Article 6(1)(f) for proportionate security, service operation and handling non-contractual correspondence; Article 6(1)(a) for processing requiring consent.

Device-storage rules may also require consent unless access is strictly necessary. Special-category data additionally needs an Article 9 condition. We cannot replace consent-required advertising with a blanket legitimate-interest claim. Required account information is needed for the relevant service; optional information and permissions can be declined, with possible limits to their features.

18. Recipients and service-provider roles

Recipients involved in the current services include Supabase for accounts, database and storage; Google for Gemini and support email; Apple for sign-in, payments, notifications and system frameworks; RevenueCat for purchase access; AppsFlyer for events and attribution; and GitHub for website hosting. Their roles depend on the processing and relevant agreements.

Necessary information may also go to authorized support personnel, professional advisers or authorities where legally required. A provider’s own policy is not a substitute for our required processing agreements. The site contains links to provider information below. We do not describe this recipient list as a claim that data remains exclusively with AMEN.

19. International transfers

Providers can operate or give authorized support access outside your country, including outside the European Economic Area. Server region, support access and further subprocessors must all be considered; choosing an EU database region does not by itself rule out every international transfer.

Where GDPR transfer rules apply, a transfer needs an applicable safeguard, such as an adequacy decision covering the recipient or appropriate contractual safeguards, including standard contractual clauses and any necessary additional measures. Contact us for information about the safeguards relevant to your data. We do not promise universal EU-only storage.

20. Retention criteria and backups

Account and synchronized content are retained to provide your account and chosen features until removed or no longer needed. Local caches depend on device and app controls. Support records depend on the request and legal obligations. Transaction records, fraud evidence and disputed matters may need longer retention under applicable law.

Hosting, AI and analytics providers apply relevant contractual and operational retention. Deleted records may remain temporarily in protected backups until replaced or expired, and legally required data may be retained with restricted access. We do not publish invented fixed deadlines where provider configuration or law determines them. You can request the criteria applicable to your records.

21. Deleting your account

The iOS app provides an account-deletion flow that requests deletion from AMEN’s backend. If it fails or you cannot access your account, contact Email AMEN. We may ask for proportionate verification so an unauthorized person cannot delete or obtain your data. Do not send a password for verification.

Uninstalling the app alone does not delete backend records. Account deletion does not cancel an Apple subscription: cancel it separately through Apple subscription cancellation. Device copies, exported content, backup expiry and records subject to lawful retention are distinct from removal of the active account.

22. Your rights and complaints

Where the GDPR applies, you can request access, correction, erasure, restriction and, where the conditions are met, portability. You may withdraw consent and object to processing based on legitimate interests for reasons relating to your situation. You may object to direct marketing at any time.

Send requests to Email AMEN. We normally respond within one month; legally permitted extensions require notice and reasons. Requests are normally free, subject to statutory exceptions. You may complain to the supervisory authority where you live, work or believe an infringement occurred. Contacting AMEN first is not a condition for complaining.

23. Security and automated processing

Data transfers use encrypted connections where supported, with access controls and other technical and organizational safeguards appropriate to the processing. No online service can guarantee absolute security. Protect your device and account, and report suspected unauthorized access.

AI generates responses and personalization, and automated checks can manage access or purchase entitlement. These functions are not presented as decisions about legal rights, credit, employment or medical treatment. If you believe an automated result significantly affects you, contact us for review and the protections applicable to that decision.

24. Changes and related documents

The date and version at the top identify this notice. We will update it when services, recipients or legal requirements change and provide additional notice where required. Publishing a revised notice does not authorize a new purpose that requires consent.

The Terms of use explain use of the service and purchases. All six language versions describe the same practices. English is the technical fallback for unavailable languages, not a waiver of local rights. Questions about this notice can be sent to Email AMEN.
